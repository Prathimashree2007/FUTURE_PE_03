## H2: What Is Teeth Whitening?

Teeth whitening is a cosmetic dental procedure intended to lighten the appearance of natural teeth.

Professional whitening treatments generally use whitening agents to reduce certain types of stains and discoloration.

The exact treatment method depends on the patient's dental condition and the type of whitening procedure recommended by the dentist.

## H2: Why Do Teeth Become Discolored?

Tooth discoloration can occur for different reasons.

### H3: Food and Drinks

Regular consumption of certain foods and drinks may contribute to surface staining.

Examples include:

- Tea
- Coffee
- Certain colored foods
- Soft drinks

### H3: Tobacco Use

Tobacco can contribute to tooth staining and discoloration.

Avoiding tobacco is also important for overall oral health.

### H3: Age

Natural changes over time can affect the appearance of teeth.

As teeth age, their outer appearance may change and underlying tooth color can become more noticeable.

### H3: Poor Oral Hygiene

Plaque and buildup can affect the appearance of teeth.

Regular brushing, cleaning between teeth, and professional dental care can help maintain oral hygiene.

### H3: Certain Types of Dental Discoloration

Not every type of discoloration responds equally to whitening.

A dentist can identify the likely cause and explain whether whitening may help.

Professional teeth whitening is usually planned after an assessment of the patient's teeth and gums.

The dentist may first examine the mouth to identify:

- Cavities
- Gum problems
- Existing restorations
- Tooth sensitivity
- Types of discoloration

If whitening is considered suitable, the dental professional can explain the recommended procedure.

The exact process depends on the whitening method being used.

## H2: Professional Teeth Whitening vs Home Whitening

There are different approaches to teeth whitening.

### H3: Professional Whitening

Professional treatment is performed or supervised by a dental professional.

It allows the dentist to assess the patient's oral condition and determine whether whitening is appropriate.

### H3: Home Whitening Products

Some whitening products are designed for home use.

However, the suitability and effectiveness of these products can vary.

If you have existing dental problems, it is useful to speak with a dental professional before starting a whitening treatment.

## H2: Is Teeth Whitening Safe?

Teeth whitening can be suitable for many people when appropriately planned and used according to professional guidance.

However, some people may experience temporary sensitivity or gum irritation.

The risk and severity can vary depending on factors such as:

- Existing tooth sensitivity
- Gum condition
- Whitening method
- Concentration of whitening agent
- Duration of treatment

A dental examination before whitening can help identify potential concerns.

---

## H2: Who May Not Be a Good Candidate for Teeth Whitening?

Whitening may not be appropriate for everyone.

A dentist may need to assess patients who have:

- Untreated cavities
- Gum problems
- Significant tooth sensitivity
- Certain types of discoloration
- Damaged teeth
- Existing dental restorations that will not change color through whitening

If you have dental concerns, treating the underlying problem may be more important than cosmetic whitening.

## H2: How Long Do Teeth Whitening Results Last?

Whitening results are not necessarily permanent.
